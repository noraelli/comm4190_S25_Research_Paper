# COMM4190 Spring 2025 - Research Paper

## LLMs in Journalism: Objectivity and Communication Implications for News Reporting

### Nora Elliot


### Overview

This paper investigates LLMs like ChatGPT are reshaping the landscape of journalism, particularly in terms of objectivity, editorial decision making, and public trust. As AI systems become increasingly embedded in news production, they challenge longstanding journalistic norms by introducing algorithmic logic into processes traditionally governed by human judgment. The paper draws upon five foundational communication theories: Agenda Setting Theory, Media Trust, Expectancy Violations Theory, Symbolic Interactionism, and Politeness Theory, to analyze how LLMs influence which and how stories are told.

Rather than portraying LLMs as inherently harmful or helpful, this paper argues that their impact hinges on how they are designed, prompted, and integrated into newsroom practices. It emphasizes the need for transparency in AI assisted reporting and highlights the evolving role of the reader, who now shapes journalistic outputs through interactive prompts. The findings suggest that while LLMs may offer benefits such as speed, factual precision, and scalability, they also risk amplifying bias, reinforcing sensationalism, and violating audience expectations if not carefully managed. Ultimately, the paper calls for editorial guidelines and accountability mechanisms to ensure that AI enhances democratic journalism rather than eroding it.
